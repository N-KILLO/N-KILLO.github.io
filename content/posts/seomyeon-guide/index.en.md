**Seomyeon (서면) is a neighborhood where you only need to remember one station: Seomyeon Station (서면역), where subway Lines 1 and 2 meet.** The pork soup alley, Seomyeon Market, the tent bar street, the underground mall and the department store are all a walk from the station, and Jeonpo Cafe Street is one stop away on Line 2. You can get here from Busan Station or from Haeundae without changing trains. The locations, sizes and times below are from the Busanjin-gu Office culture and tourism pages, Visit Busan and Busan Transportation Corporation, checked on November 22, 2026.

Seomyeon has no headline sight like a beach or an observatory. What it has is alleys for eating, shopping and drinking, packed tightly around the station. So it fits an itinerary better as **one dinner and a night walk** than as a half-day attraction.

## Seomyeon Station: where Lines 1 and 2 meet

Busan subway Line 1 (Dadaepo Beach–Nopo) and Line 2 (Jangsan–Yangsan) cross at Seomyeon Station. Anyone heading from the Busan Station side to Haeundae or Gwangalli passes through it at least once.

| Good to know at Seomyeon Station | Details |
|---|---|
| Lines | Transfer between Line 1 and Line 2 |
| Lost and found center | B2, in the Line 1–2 transfer passage (weekdays 09:00–18:00, 051-640-7339) |
| Underground mall | The station connects directly to Seomyeon Underground Shopping Center, Seomyeon Mall |
| Last Line 2 train toward Jangsan (Haeundae direction) | 00:00 |

The station is big and has a lot of exits. Don't try to memorize exit numbers. Type the name of the shop or alley into a map app and tap walking directions, and the nearest exit comes up with the route. You'll get lost less if you pick the exit while you're still underground.

## Where the Seomyeon food alleys are

### Seomyeon Pork Soup Alley

The pork soup restaurants are clustered between numbers 16 and 35 on Seomyeon-ro 68beon-gil. According to the Busanjin-gu Office, the street is one block west of Jungang-daero. Seomyeon Market (56 Seomyeon-ro) is right there, so do both together.

If you're going to eat pork soup exactly once in Busan, this is a safe pick. Several restaurants sit side by side, so if one has a long line you can walk into the next.

### The noodle alley in Seomyeon Market

Seomyeon Market is a two-story market that opened in its modern form in 1970. It has about 190 shops: around 120 on the 1st floor, a mix of dried seafood stores, imported food stores and restaurants, and around 70 on the 2nd floor, such as clothing alteration shops. **The knife-cut noodle (kalguksu) alley and the gukbap alley on the 1st floor** are the parts that show up on Korean TV.

### Seomyeon Tent Bar Street

About 50 tent bars (pojangmacha) line a 370 m stretch along the back gate of Lotte Department Store and Lotte Hotel and the side street to the east. These are the old-style ones, orange tarps with bare light bulbs. Ask what the dishes cost before you sit down, and bring some cash so you don't have to think about it.

### Seomyeon Munhwa-ro Food Street

This street runs 550 m from Seomyeon Station Line 2, Exit 9 to the underpass beneath the Donghae Line tracks. A small stream runs along it, with ginkgo trees on the side. There are about 100 restaurants here: around 10 raw fish places, plus Korean beef bulgogi, samgyetang, Korean set meals, Chinese and Japanese. Youngkwang Bookstore (영광도서, 10 Seomyeon Munhwa-ro), which opened in 1967, is on this street too.

If you don't like noisy alleys, or you're traveling with your parents, come here instead of the tent bar street.

### Youth Street and Seomyeon 1beon-ga

The area around Judies Taehwa (쥬디스태화) is divided into Youth Street, a food street and an academy street, with sculptures in three plazas. Seomyeon 1beon-ga (서면1번가) is the name for a 420 m cross-shaped stretch of back streets. Bars, restaurants and cafes are at their densest here, so if you want a quiet meal, just walk through and look.

## Jeonpo Cafe Street

{{< figure src="jeonpo-cafe-street.jpg" alt="An alley on Jeonpo Cafe Street with small goods shops and cafes on the ground floors of brick buildings" caption="An alley on Jeonpo Cafe Street. It is a 6-minute walk from Jeonpo Station Line 2, Exit 7 (Visit Busan guide, November 2026) (October 2020) · Photo: Korea Tourism Organization (PhoKo), Korea Tourism Organization, Lee Beom-su · KOGL Type 1" >}}

Jeonpo Cafe Street is the group of alleys around Jeonpo Catholic Church (Jeonpo-daero 209beon-gil and 223beon-gil, Seojeon-ro 38beon-gil and 46beon-gil). The area used to be a hardware and tool district, and about 170 cafes, restaurants and handicraft shops have moved in. In 2017 the New York Times put it on its "52 Places to Go" list for the year. Around 300 tool shops are still in business, so you get cafes with tool shops next door.

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Address (reference point) | 26 Jeonpo-daero 209beon-gil, Busanjin-gu (부산진구 전포대로209번길 26) |
| Subway | 6-minute walk from Jeonpo Station Line 2, Exit 7 |
| Bus | Get off at the Bujeon Library stop, 2-minute walk (5-1, 10, 20, 29, 43, 52, 57, 80, 99, 111, 133, 169-1) |
| Parking | Public parking lots nearby |

Jeonpo Station is one stop from Seomyeon Station on Line 2. Cafes are best in the daytime and the tent bars and pubs at night, so the route that works is **coffee in Jeonpo in the afternoon, then over to Seomyeon for the evening**.

## Shopping: underground mall, Bujeon Market, department store

- **Seomyeon Underground Shopping Center, Seomyeon Mall** (중앙대로 지하717, 051-713-8297): The underground mall connected to Seomyeon Station. Lots of clothing, cosmetics, shoe and bag shops. It also connects to basement level 1 of Judies Taehwa. On a rainy day, this is how you get around Seomyeon.
- **Bujeon Market Town (Bujeon Market)** (23 Jungang-daero 783beon-gil, 051-818-9454): Seven markets grouped together, including Bujeon Market, Bujeon Ginseng Market and Busan Electronics Market, with about 3,000 shops. Hours are **04:00–19:00**. By evening the stalls are closing up, so go in the morning or early afternoon.
- **Lotte Department Store Busan Main Branch** (롯데백화점 부산본점, 772 Gaya-daero, 051-810-2500): Parking is ₩2,000 for the first 30 minutes, then ₩500 per 10 minutes.
- **Samjung Tower** (삼정타워, 672 Jungang-daero): Parking is free for the first 30 minutes, then ₩1,000 per 10 minutes, with free time added depending on how much you spend.

Parking here is charged in 10-minute steps. Leave the car at your hotel and take the subway.

## When you want to walk: Busan Citizens Park

Busan Citizens Park (73 Simingongwon-ro / 시민공원로 73) was built on the site of the former US Army base Camp Hialeah. It covers 473,279 m², and the lawn plaza in the middle alone is about 40,000 m², the size of six soccer fields. By subway, use **Bujeon Station Line 1, Exit 7**. The parking lot has 902 spaces, and the enquiry number is 051-850-6000.

It's a good place to walk for an hour between meals and drinks. If you're here with kids, you'll probably spend more time in the park than in the Seomyeon alleys.

## How to get to Seomyeon

| From | How | Fare |
|---|---|---|
| Busan Station | Line 1 toward Nopo, no transfer | ₩1,600 with a transit card |
| Haeundae Station | Line 2 toward Yangsan/Hopo, no transfer | ₩1,600–1,800 with a transit card (by distance section) |
| Gimhae Airport | Light rail → at Sasang Station, Line 2 toward Jangsan, about 26 minutes | ₩1,600 with a transit card |
| Gimhae Airport | Limousine bus bound for Seomyeon/Bujeon (12 a day) | ₩6,000 |

- A taxi from Gimhae Airport takes 45 minutes and costs around ₩18,000, according to the airport's guide. For three people that's the same as the limousine bus (₩6,000 × 3), and with four the taxi is cheaper.
- **Last trains**: The last Line 2 train from Haeundae Station toward Seomyeon is at 23:29, and the last Line 1 train from Busan Station toward Seomyeon is at 23:49. Going back to Haeundae, the last Line 2 train from Seomyeon toward Jangsan is at 00:00.

If you're staying out late in Seomyeon and sleeping in Haeundae, head down to the station by 23:30. Miss the midnight train and a taxi is the only option. ## A half-day route in Seomyeon

1. Coffee on **Jeonpo Cafe Street** in the afternoon (Jeonpo Station, Exit 7)
2. Cross over to Seomyeon and browse the **underground mall** or the area around Judies Taehwa
3. Dinner in the **pork soup alley** or the **noodle alley in Seomyeon Market**
4. Second round on the **tent bar street**, or **Seomyeon Munhwa-ro** if you want it quieter
5. Be at Seomyeon Station before 23:30

If you want to fit in Bujeon Market, flip the order and start at the market in the morning. It closes at 19:00.

## FAQ

### Which subway line is Seomyeon on?

It's a transfer station for Lines 1 and 2. From Busan Station take Line 1, and from Haeundae or Gwangalli take Line 2. Neither needs a transfer.

### Where is the Seomyeon pork soup alley?

Between 16 and 35 Seomyeon-ro 68beon-gil. It's easy to find if you search for Seomyeon Market (서면시장) in a map app.

### For Jeonpo Cafe Street, do I get off at Seomyeon Station or Jeonpo Station?

Jeonpo Station. It's a 6-minute walk from Line 2, Exit 7. Jeonpo is one stop from Seomyeon Station on Line 2.

### What time does Bujeon Market close?

Hours are 04:00–19:00, according to the Busanjin-gu Office. Go in the morning, not the evening.

### What time is the last train from Seomyeon to Haeundae?

The last Line 2 train from Seomyeon Station toward Jangsan is at 00:00.
