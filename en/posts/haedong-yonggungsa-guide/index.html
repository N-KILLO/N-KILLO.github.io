Haedong Yonggungsa Temple (해동용궁사) is free to enter and opens at 04:30 every day of the year. Parking is ₩3,000 for a car. By public transport, take bus 139 or 1001 from Osiria Station (오시리아역) on the Donghae Line, or bus 181 from Haeundae Station (해운대역). The temple sits on seaside rocks instead of up a mountain, which is why it ends up on almost every Busan itinerary. Everything below is from the official Haedong Yonggungsa website and Visit Busan, the city’s official tourism portal, checked on November 26, 2026.

Haedong Yonggungsa entrance fee and opening hours

There is no entrance fee. The temple’s website says “Admission free” and “Open all year, worship possible 365 days.” The only thing you pay for is parking.

The opening hours differ a little depending on where you look.

SourceOpensCloses
Haedong Yonggungsa websiteEntry from 04:30Exit by 20:30
Visit Busan04:3019:20 (entry until 18:50)

Both agree on the 04:30 opening. For the evening, go by the earlier one and plan to be inside before 18:50, and you won’t make the trip for nothing. In winter the sun is down a little after 5 anyway (sunset in Busan in early December is 17:11, per the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute), so arrive before 4 p.m. if you want to see the colour of the sea. Hours can change over the big holidays, so call 051-722-7744 if you are going then.

What kind of temple is it? The order to walk it

Haedong Yonggungsa was founded in 1376, the second year of King U of Goryeo, by the monk Naong Hwasang. It burned down during the Imjin War and was rebuilt in the early 1930s. In the early 1970s the monk Jeongam is said to have dreamed of Gwanseeum-bosal (the Bodhisattva of Compassion) riding a dragon up into the sky, and then gave the temple its current name. The temple presents itself as one of Korea’s three major Gwaneum holy sites, along with Naksansa in Yangyang and Boriam in Namhae.

This is the order you walk it in.

  1. Twelve zodiac statues: the first thing you meet at the entrance. People take a photo in front of their own zodiac animal.
  2. Iljumun gate and the 108 steps: go down the steps between the pine trees and the sea and the temple come into view together.
  3. Yongmun Bridge: the stone bridge that crosses over to the temple. A lot of people throw a coin and make a wish here.
  4. Daeungbojeon main hall: the first stop in the worship order the temple recommends. Podae Hwasang and Yonggungdan come next.
  5. Haesu Gwaneum Daebul (the great Gwaneum statue): up the steps beside the main hall. From here you look down on the temple and the sea at once.
  6. Sunrise Rock and the coastal walking path: a seaside path that is part of Haeparang Trail Course 1. It runs on toward the National Institute of Fisheries Science (국립수산과학원).

There are a lot of steps. You go down the 108 steps on the way in and climb them again on the way out, so wear comfortable trainers. When you go into a prayer hall, use the side door, not the front one (that is for monks only), and leave your shoes neatly outside.

Sunrise at Haedong Yonggungsa

Because the gate opens at 04:30, you can get in and find a spot before the sun comes up. Sunrise in Busan on December 31 is at 07:32 (Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute, checked November 11, 2026). In December the sun rises after 7, so arriving around 7 is enough. It is right on the sea and the wind comes straight in, so bring a thick padded jacket for the early morning. What to wear is covered in the Busan in December clothing post.

How to get there

① Osiria Station, Donghae Line (the easiest) Come out of Exit 1 of Osiria Station and take bus 139 or express bus 1001 from the Osiria Station stop. Get off at “Yonggungsa·National Institute of Fisheries Science (용궁사·국립수산과학원)” and walk about 15 minutes. You can transfer to the Donghae Line at BEXCO Station (벡스코역) on Line 2.

② Bus 181 from Haeundae Station This is the route the temple’s website gives. Take bus 181 from Haeundae Station on Line 2, get off at “Yonggungsa Entrance” and walk from there. Bus 181 runs every 20 minutes. For Busan Station to Haeundae, see Busan Station to Haeundae.

③ From Songjeong Beach You can also catch bus 181 or village bus 9 (Gijang-gun) at the entrance to Songjeong Beach (송정해수욕장). It is close enough to head over after some time at Songjeong.

If you have luggage or there are three or more of you, a taxi from Haeundae or Songjeong is less hassle, because the bus still leaves you with a 15-minute walk. For bus arrival times, tap the stop name in Naver Map before you set off and it shows them live.

Parking at Haedong Yonggungsa

The car park in front of the temple is paid. The temple’s website says “The parking fee has nothing to do with the temple,” meaning the money does not go to the temple.

VehicleParking fee
Up to 15 seats₩3,000
16 seats or more (buses)₩5,000–7,000 depending on size

For a regular car, count on ₩3,000. On weekend afternoons and before dawn on New Year’s Day the road in is jammed from the start, so on those days I’d get off at Osiria Station and take the bus.

Tips

  • Go in the morning. The temple faces the sea to the east, so the water looks its best before noon.
  • Pets can come in with you in a carrier or wearing a muzzle.
  • The National Institute of Fisheries Science is connected by the coastal walking path. It pairs well if you are going with kids.
  • Starting from Haeundae, riding the Haeundae Sky Capsule in the morning and coming over in the afternoon also works.

FAQ

Is there an entrance fee at Haedong Yonggungsa?

No. It is free. You only pay for parking (₩3,000 for a car).

How late can I get in?

Visit Busan says entry is until 18:50. The temple’s website gives 8:30 p.m. as the exit time. Get in before 18:50 and you are safe.

Is it ever closed?

No. It is open all year.

Are there a lot of steps?

Yes. You go down 108 steps from the entrance to the temple and have to climb them again on the way back.
